Canada’s other Black Friday: the day the Avro Arrow was cancelled

Black Friday brings to mind bargains and Cyber Monday sales. But in Canadian aviation, those same two words recall February 20, 1959, when a remarkable Canadian aircraft programme came to an abrupt end.

What happened on Canada’s Black Friday in 1959?

On Friday, February 20, 1959, Prime Minister John Diefenbaker announced that Canada would end the Avro CF-105 Arrow programme and its companion Orenda Iroquois engine project. The Arrow was a supersonic interceptor built in Malton, Ontario, to defend North America against Soviet bombers during the Cold War.

The aircraft had made its first flight on March 25, 1958. Its elegant delta wings and advanced electronic flight controls were a striking demonstration of Canadian engineering. Five aircraft had flown, and another was being prepared with Canadian-designed Iroquois engines. But escalating costs and changing defence priorities, including the growing importance of missiles, made its future uncertain.

Roughly 14,000 Avro employees lost their jobs in the immediate aftermath, and suppliers were affected as well. Completed aircraft and unfinished airframes were subsequently scrapped. Some former Avro engineers went on to work in the American space programme. The decision remains debated by historians, engineers and aviation enthusiasts.

That is why February 20, 1959 is remembered in Canadian aviation as Black Friday. It was not a shopping holiday, but the end of a major aerospace undertaking.

How did the shopping Black Friday get its name?

The retail Black Friday is the Friday after American Thanksgiving. Accounts trace its shopping-related name to the Philadelphia area around the late 1950s and early 1960s, when the post-Thanksgiving rush meant crowded streets and difficult traffic for police. Written evidence from 1961 indicates that the expression was already in use there.

Later, retailers helped recast the name as a positive one, linking it with the idea of moving from losses (red ink) to profits (black ink). That is a later explanation, not the best-supported origin of the shopping term. Nor did the retail event take its name from the Avro Arrow cancellation. The overlap is a coincidence: two different events, with the same expression gaining currency in roughly the same historical period.

Where does Cyber Monday fit in?

Cyber Monday is the Monday following American Thanksgiving. In 2005, the National Retail Federation’s digital retail division, Shop.org, popularized the name after noticing increased online shopping on that Monday. In 2026, Black Friday falls on November 27 and Cyber Monday on November 30.

Those are modern shopping traditions. Canada's October Thanksgiving does not determine their dates, and neither shopping event is connected to the Arrow’s February anniversary. If you landed here looking for a little Canadian history between sales, now you know why an aviation enthusiast might hear “Black Friday” and think of a jet instead of a checkout cart.

Black Friday and the Avro Arrow: quick answers

Was the Avro Arrow cancelled on the same Black Friday as the shopping event?

No. Its cancellation took place on February 20, 1959. Retail Black Friday occurs in November after American Thanksgiving.

When is the Avro Arrow Black Friday anniversary?

February 20 every year, regardless of which day of the week it falls on.

Did the Avro Arrow inspire the name Black Friday or Cyber Monday?

No known historical connection links the Canadian aviation nickname to either retail term. Cyber Monday was introduced in 2005, decades after the Arrow programme ended.
